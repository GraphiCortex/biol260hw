# CDC14B circRNA–miRNA regulatory hypothesis in breast cancer

A reproducible BIOL 260 bioinformatics project built around the assigned cell-cycle gene **CDC14B**.

The original exercise asks for a gene → circRNA → miRNA → mRNA regulatory axis in breast cancer. Rather than treating those steps as unrelated database lookups, this repository develops them as one biological question:

> **Could a CDC14B-derived circular RNA plausibly interact with a breast-cancer-relevant miRNA and thereby alter repression of downstream mRNA targets?**

The result is a **hypothesis-generating computational model**, not a demonstrated molecular pathway.

---

## Final proposed axis

```text
CDC14B
  |
  | back-splicing / circRNA derivation
  v
hsa_circ_0087640
  |
  | putative miRNA sequestration
  v
miR-136-5p
  |
  | predicted miRNA-mediated repression
  v
ORC6 / XRCC2 / TFAP2C
```

Compactly:

```text
CDC14B -> hsa_circ_0087640 -| miR-136-5p -| {ORC6, XRCC2, TFAP2C}
```

The inhibitory edges are **predicted regulatory relationships**. This project does not experimentally establish that `hsa_circ_0087640` physically sponges miR-136-5p or that the circRNA derepresses the three downstream genes in breast-cancer cells.

---

## Biological idea

A **circular RNA (circRNA)** is generated by back-splicing, in which a downstream splice donor is joined to an upstream splice acceptor to form a covalently closed RNA molecule. Because a circRNA has its own mature sequence and back-splice junction, its regulatory interactions cannot simply be inferred from the linear host-gene mRNA.

A **microRNA (miRNA)** is a short regulatory RNA. Nucleotides 2–8 form the **seed region**, which is a major determinant of target recognition. When a miRNA binds a compatible response element on an mRNA, it can contribute to repression of that transcript.

Some circRNAs contain miRNA-compatible sequence motifs. If the circRNA is sufficiently abundant, accessible, and present in the appropriate cellular compartment, it may compete for a miRNA and reduce the amount available to repress other targets. This is commonly described as **miRNA sponging**.

A sequence match alone does **not** prove sponging. Expression level, localization, RNA structure, AGO association, and molecular stoichiometry all matter.

## Why CDC14B?

For BIOL 260 Section B2, **CDC14B** was the assigned gene for students whose names begin with J–O.

CDC14B is a dual-specificity phosphatase involved in cell-cycle control and DNA-damage checkpoint regulation, making it biologically appropriate for an exercise centered on breast-tissue tumorigenesis and cell-cycle regulation.

Two CDC14B-derived circRNAs were retained:

- `hsa_circ_0087640`
- `hsa_circ_0087641`

`hsa_circ_0087640` was used for downstream sequence analysis because its mature circRNA sequence was available.

---

## Why this analysis went beyond the minimum exercise

The CircInteractome page linked in the assignment was unavailable during the analysis. Instead of selecting miRNAs manually or replacing the requested logic with an unrelated predictor, the intended sequence-based screening step was reconstructed programmatically.

That made it possible to:

- inspect the mature circRNA sequence directly;
- search canonical miRNA seed sites;
- include sites crossing the **back-splice junction**;
- collapse mature miRNAs that share the same nt2–8 seed;
- compare sequence support before consulting cancer literature;
- query TargetScan reproducibly through `multiMiR`;
- diagnose an old/new miRNA nomenclature problem for miR-217;
- compare candidate target networks and test whether apparent GO enrichment was robust to the background definition.

The purpose of the extra analysis was therefore **not to manufacture a stronger result**, but to reduce cherry-picking and make the limits of the evidence visible.

## Stage 1 — circRNA sequence scan

The mature sequence of `hsa_circ_0087640` is stored in:

```text
Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640.fasta
```

Human mature-miRNA sequences come from the pinned miRBase v22.1 FASTA:

```text
Data/miRBase_v22.1_mature_Homo_sapiens.fasta
```

The script

```text
Scripts/01_scan_cdc14b_circrna_mirna.py
```

searches three canonical TargetScan-style site classes:

- **8mer** — complementarity to miRNA nt2–8 plus the A1 feature;
- **7mer-m8** — complementarity to miRNA nt2–8;
- **7mer-A1** — complementarity to miRNA nt2–7 plus the A1 feature.

Because the RNA is circular, the scan also checks for sites that cross the written end/beginning of the sequence, i.e. the **back-splice junction**.

The initial scan identified hundreds of mature miRNAs with at least one canonical sequence match. Those raw matches are stored in:

```text
Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan.csv
Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan_sites.csv
```

### Sequence-ranking heuristic

For transparent prioritization, canonical sites were summarized using:

```text
S_seed = 3*(8mer) + 2*(7mer-m8) + 1*(7mer-A1)
```

This is a simple ranking heuristic used within this project. It is **not** a validated free-energy model or a probability that binding occurs in vivo.

## Stage 2 — seed-family collapse

Different mature miRNAs can share the same nt2–8 seed sequence. From the point of view of a seed-only circRNA scan, such miRNAs are not independent sequence hypotheses.

`Scripts/02_collapse_seed_families.py` therefore collapses mature miRNAs into seed families and produces:

```text
Data/stage2/
├── seed_family_summary.csv
├── sequence_shortlist.csv
└── junction_spanning_sites.csv
```

This prevents a large miRNA family from appearing artificially important simply because many family members encode the same seed.

## Candidate miRNAs

The final biological comparison focused on:

| Candidate | Sequence score | Why it remained interesting |
|---|---:|---|
| `hsa-miR-217-5p` | **7** | strongest sequence-derived finalist; independent breast-cancer tumor-suppressive evidence |
| `hsa-miR-106a-3p` | 5 | linked to mammary epithelial plasticity and tumoroid-initiation biology |
| `hsa-miR-136-5p` | 5 | breast-cancer relevance, prior circRNA-sponging precedent, and METABRIC availability |

The broader sequence-ranked set was also retained for TargetScan/network comparison rather than discarded immediately.

### Why miR-136-5p became the formal homework candidate

`miR-217-5p` was the strongest sequence-derived candidate. However, the homework also requires a breast-cancer Kaplan–Meier analysis. In the current KM Plotter breast-miRNA interface, among the three finalists only the older identifier `hsa-miR-136` was available in the assignment-specified **METABRIC** cohort.

Therefore `miR-136-5p` was used for the formal axis because it could be followed consistently through all required evidence layers.

That is a practical evidence-integration choice, **not a claim that miR-136-5p is the uniquely strongest binder of the circRNA**.

## Stage 3 — TargetScan predictions through multiMiR

Direct TargetScan access proved unreliable during development, so the successful reproducible route used the Bioconductor package **multiMiR**, querying only its TargetScan table.

The successful Stage-3 outputs are in:

```text
Data/stage3_multimir/
```

This folder contains candidate-specific TargetScan files, the queried-candidate table, database metadata, the combined raw TargetScan output, and the failed/unmapped record.

The main script is:

```text
Scripts/03_targetscan_multimir.R
```

It:

1. reads the Stage-2 sequence shortlist;
2. queries the top sequence-ranked candidates;
3. restricts the query to TargetScan;
4. keeps the pre-specified top 20% of TargetScan predictions;
5. exports individual and combined results.

### miR-217 nomenclature problem

TargetScan/multiMiR did not return records for the newer identifier:

```text
hsa-miR-217-5p
```

A dedicated nomenclature check showed that the older TargetScan-style identifier:

```text
hsa-miR-217
```

did return predictions.

That diagnostic is preserved in:

```text
Scripts/03b_rescue_mir217_targetscan.R
Data/stage3b_mir217/
```

The matched top-20% miR-217 predictions were then added to the other candidates using:

```text
Scripts/03c_integrate_mir217.R
```

producing the final combined TargetScan dataset in:

```text
Data/stage3_complete/
├── 01_hsa-miR-217-5p_TargetScan.csv
└── targetscan_combined_with_mir217.csv
```

## Stage 4 — gene-level target networks and GO sensitivity analysis

TargetScan can return multiple records associated with the same miRNA–gene pair. `Scripts/04_target_network_go.R` collapses those records to gene-level evidence and computes:

- unique predicted target counts;
- pairwise target overlap;
- Jaccard similarity between target sets;
- GO Biological Process enrichment;
- a pre-specified summary of cancer-relevant biological themes.

The first network analysis, before miR-217 was reintegrated, is stored in:

```text
Data/stage4/
```

The final six-candidate analysis is stored in:

```text
Data/stage4_complete/
```

Key final outputs include:

```text
Data/stage4_complete/
├── candidate_target_summary.csv
├── targetscan_gene_level.csv
├── target_overlap_counts.csv
├── target_overlap_jaccard.csv
├── go_bp_enrichment_human_background.csv
├── go_bp_enrichment_targetscan_union_background.csv
├── go_bp_top_terms.csv
└── cancer_theme_summary.csv
```

### Why the GO analysis is treated cautiously

Some apparently interesting GO terms appeared when the background was taken to be all human GO-annotated genes.

However, those pathway-level signals were not robust when the background was changed to the union of genes represented in the TargetScan candidate sets.

For that reason, GO enrichment was used as a **robustness check**, not as evidence for a claimed mechanism. No pathway was selected simply because it produced an attractive enrichment result.

## Kaplan–Meier analysis

The survival analysis was performed manually in the KM Plotter breast-miRNA interface because that is the framework specified by the course exercise.

Settings:

```text
miRNA:              hsa-miR-136
dataset:            METABRIC
endpoint:           overall survival (OS)
patient split:      median
follow-up:          unrestricted
clinical filters:   none
```

Result:

```text
N = 1262
low expression  = 637
high expression = 625

HR = 0.84
95% CI = 0.69–1.02
log-rank p = 0.081
```

Higher miR-136 expression therefore showed a **directionally favorable but statistically non-significant** association with overall survival.

The point estimate corresponds to an approximately 16% lower estimated hazard in the high-expression group, but:

```text
p = 0.081 > 0.05
```

and the 95% confidence interval includes 1.

The analysis is therefore reported as a **non-significant trend**, not as evidence that miR-136 is a validated prognostic biomarker.

The Kaplan–Meier figure and its interpretation are included in the final report:

- [`BIOL260_CDC14B_report.pdf`](BIOL260_CDC14B_report.pdf)

## Reproducing the final computational analysis

Run the commands from the repository root.

### Requirements

- Python 3
- R
- internet access for the `multiMiR` database query
- R/Bioconductor packages used by the scripts:
  - `multiMiR`
  - `AnnotationDbi`
  - `org.Hs.eg.db`
  - `GO.db`

The Python sequence scripts use only the Python standard library.

### 1. Scan `hsa_circ_0087640`

```bash
python Scripts/01_scan_cdc14b_circrna_mirna.py \
  --circ-fasta Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640.fasta \
  --mirna-fasta Data/miRBase_v22.1_mature_Homo_sapiens.fasta \
  --out Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan.csv \
  --top 0
```

The site-level output is generated automatically as:

```text
Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan_sites.csv
```

### 2. Collapse identical seed families

```bash
python Scripts/02_collapse_seed_families.py \
  --summary Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan.csv \
  --sites Data/cdc14b_circ_0087640_mirna_scan_sites.csv \
  --outdir Data/stage2
```

### 3. Query TargetScan through multiMiR

```bash
Rscript Scripts/03_targetscan_multimir.R \
  Data/stage2/sequence_shortlist.csv \
  Data/stage3_multimir \
  6
```

This initial query produces five successful TargetScan candidate sets; miR-217 requires the older TargetScan identifier.

### 4. Add miR-217 using the matched top-20% criterion

```bash
Rscript Scripts/03c_integrate_mir217.R \
  Data/stage3_multimir/targetscan_combined_raw.csv \
  Data/stage3_complete
```

For the diagnostic alias check itself:

```bash
Rscript Scripts/03b_rescue_mir217_targetscan.R \
  Data/stage3b_mir217
```

### 5. Run the final six-candidate target-network analysis

```bash
Rscript Scripts/04_target_network_go.R \
  Data/stage3_complete/targetscan_combined_with_mir217.csv \
  Data/stage4_complete
```

## What the project supports

The analysis supports the following statements:

- `hsa_circ_0087640` is a CDC14B-derived circRNA with a mature sequence that can be screened computationally;
- its sequence contains canonical seed-compatible sites for multiple human miRNAs;
- `miR-136-5p` is one plausible candidate with independent relevance to breast-cancer biology;
- TargetScan predicts `ORC6`, `XRCC2`, and `TFAP2C` among its possible downstream targets;
- higher miR-136 expression shows a directionally favorable, but non-significant, overall-survival trend in METABRIC.

The project does **not** establish that:

- `hsa_circ_0087640` physically binds miR-136-5p in breast-cancer cells;
- the circRNA functions as an effective miRNA sponge in vivo;
- altering the circRNA changes `ORC6`, `XRCC2`, or `TFAP2C`;
- miR-136 is an independently validated prognostic biomarker;
- the exploratory GO results demonstrate a causal pathway.

---

## What would validate the hypothesis experimentally?

A reasonable follow-up would include:

1. junction-specific RT-qPCR to confirm `hsa_circ_0087640`;
2. RNase R resistance and/or sequencing across the back-splice junction;
3. subcellular localization of the circRNA;
4. AGO2-RIP, RNA pull-down, or luciferase assays for circRNA–miRNA binding;
5. circRNA/miRNA perturbation followed by measurement of `ORC6`, `XRCC2`, and `TFAP2C`;
6. rescue experiments to test whether the predicted relationships are causal.

---

## Main limitations

- The CircInteractome URL supplied in the course exercise was unavailable during the analysis, so the intended sequence-screening logic was reconstructed programmatically.
- The miRCancer interface was not reliably accessible; breast-cancer dysregulation direction was therefore checked against primary literature in the report.
- TargetScan predictions are computational and do not prove direct regulation.
- Among the three final miRNA candidates, only `hsa-miR-136` was available in the assignment-specified METABRIC miRNA interface at the time of analysis.
- The METABRIC survival result did not reach conventional statistical significance (`p = 0.081`).
- GO enrichment was sensitive to the choice of background universe and was therefore treated as exploratory.
